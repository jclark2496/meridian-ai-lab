Evenings that earn their keep

A spring packing list for two nights under canvas — what we take, what we leave, and why the second cup of coffee always tastes better outside.

There is a particular hour between dinner and dark when a campsite decides what kind of night it will be. The fire either catches clean or it doesn’t. The tent either stands quiet in the wind or it talks all night. We have spent twenty-eight years chasing the first kind of evening.

This spring’s Camp Edit is built around that hour. Not the summit photo. Not the mileage brag. The part where you sit down, pour something hot, and realize the gear disappeared the way it was supposed to.

Shelter that stays put

For two people in wet weather, we still reach for the Meridian 2P. Full-coverage fly, taped seams, a floor rated to 3,000mm. It is not the lightest tent we have ever weighed. It is the one we trust when the forecast lies.

“Take the tent you will be glad you carried when the rain starts at 2 a.m.”

If you are staying closer to the truck, the canvas 4P changes the whole mood of camp — stove-safe vestibule, room to stand, the smell of waxed cloth after a season on the road.

What goes in the pack

Field Pack 32 for overnight. Daylight Daypack for the day hike out of camp. Range Shell over a Thermal Fleece when the front moves through. Tin Cup Flask for the coffee that makes the whole system make sense.

Leave the extras. Bring the repair kit. And if you are unsure, ask the Camp Concierge — or stop by a flagship and we will pack the bag with you on the floor.
